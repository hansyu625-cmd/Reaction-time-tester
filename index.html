        How it adds up

        Each test turns your result into points from 1 to 99 on a smooth curve, where 50 points is a typical result. Your Reflex Score is the average of the four, from one full run.

        The σ figure says how far that is from typical, in standard deviations: +1σ beats about 84 in 100 people, +2σ about 98. The typical values are our estimates from common results on tests like these, not a survey, so read it as a fair comparison between players here.

        Help

        How do I make an account?

        Choose Sign up, pick a username and a password of at least 8 characters. Sign in with them on any device and your scores come with you.

        Can I play without one?

        Yes. Every test works without an account and your bests stay in this browser. Results from this visit are saved to your account as soon as you sign up or sign in.

        What does +1.2σ mean?

        It's how far your score sits from typical, in standard deviations. 0σ is exactly typical. +1σ beats roughly 84 in 100 people and +2σ roughly 98 in 100.

        How safe is my password?

        Passwords are handled by Supabase, the service that runs the accounts. They're sent over an encrypted connection and stored only as a secure hash, never as typed. Even so, this is a game, not a bank: use a password you don't use anywhere else.

        I forgot my password

        There's no email on file, so it can't be reset from here. Ask the person who runs Reflex Score to reset it for you, or sign up again with a new username.

        Why can't I paste in number memory?

        Because it would test your clipboard, not your memory. The number is drawn as a picture, so it can't be selected, and the answer box ignores pasted or dropped text.

        Why can't I save my scores?

        Saving needs you signed in. Results you play as a guest stay in this browser and are saved to your account as soon as you sign up or sign in during the same visit.

        Could someone fake a score?

        The server works out the points itself and rejects impossible results, but tests are still timed in each player's own browser, so a determined person could fake one. Treat the board as a friendly competition.
